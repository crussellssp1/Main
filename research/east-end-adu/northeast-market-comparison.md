# NORTHEAST ADU MARKETS — WHERE IS THIS EASIER THAN THE HAMPTONS? (1 OF 1)

**Prepared:** September 23, 2026
**Subject:** Comparative regulatory and commercial screen of Northeast ADU markets against the East End baseline
**Companion to:** `market-research.md` (Sept 18) and `plinth-labs-assessment.md` (Sept 23)

---

## 1. WHAT REALLY MATTERS

- **Massachusetts is a different universe, and the gap is not close.** ADUs have been legal by right statewide since February 2, 2025 under 760 CMR 71.00. Every city and town must permit an ADU up to 900 sq ft with no special permit and no public hearing. **Municipalities are prohibited from requiring owner-occupancy of either unit, and there is no rent cap, no tenant income test and no tenant employment test.** Over 1,200 ADUs were approved in the first year.
- **The East End is arguably the single hardest ADU market in the Northeast.** East Hampton caps detached units at 600 sq ft, caps permits at 200 town-wide and 40 per school district, caps rent at 130% of HUD Fair Market Rent, restricts tenancy by residency, and requires annual permit renewal. Southampton adds a 30,000 sq ft lot minimum and a tenant work test of 30 hours per week at a local business. Sag Harbor Village's lot thresholds block six of every eight applications.
- **The economics shift more than the paperwork does.** A 900 sq ft Massachusetts unit at market rent against a 600 sq ft East Hampton unit at a capped $3,092 per month is roughly double the revenue ceiling on perhaps 40% more cost. For a design-build operator the contribution per contract rises from about $66,000 to about $95,000, which **cuts breakeven from roughly eight units per year to roughly five.**
- **Wastewater follows you.** Cape Cod is not an escape from Suffolk County Article 6. MassDEP designated 31 Cape watersheds as Natural Resource Nitrogen Sensitive Areas effective July 7, 2023, requiring Best Available Nitrogen Reducing Technology unless the town holds a watershed permit. The genuinely easy markets on this axis are **sewered**, which is why seven of the ten highest-volume Massachusetts ADU communities in 2025 were sewered cities and inner suburbs.
- **Nantucket is the direct Sag Harbor analog with a vastly better legal regime.** Median single-family price $3.7 million, the highest in Massachusetts. 60% of island homes seasonal or short-term. Historic district design review as strict as anything on the East End. And it ranked third in the entire Commonwealth with 27 ADU approvals in 2025, on an island of roughly 14,000 year-round residents.

## 2. HOW TO SCORE AN ADU MARKET

Eight variables determine whether a market is executable. The East End fails six of them.

| # | Variable | Why it matters | East End |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | By-right vs discretionary | Discretionary review kills schedule certainty and makes a product impossible | Mixed; village work routes through BHPAR |
| 2 | Statutory permit caps | A cap is a hard ceiling on lifetime revenue | East Hampton 200 town-wide |
| 3 | Maximum unit size | Fixed costs spread over more sq ft; larger unit commands more rent | 600 sq ft detached |
| 4 | Owner-occupancy mandate | Determines whether investors can be customers | Required in Southampton |
| 5 | Rent cap and tenant tests | Caps revenue and adds a permanent compliance burden | 130% HUD FMR plus work and residency tests |
| 6 | Wastewater regime | Sewer vs septic vs nitrogen-sensitive septic is the largest cost variance | Article 6, I/A OWTS, worst case |
| 7 | Financing availability | The purchase is financing-led | CHF, Plus One, thinly funded |
| 8 | Competitive density | Easy markets attract entrants | Almost no design-led competition |

**The East End's one genuine advantage is number eight.** Nobody credible is competing on architecture there. Everywhere else in this memo, somebody is.

## 4. THE MASSACHUSETTS CASE IN DETAIL

### 4.1 What the law actually does

The Affordable Homes Act was signed August 6, 2024, authorizing $5.16 billion over five years across nearly 50 policy initiatives. The implementing ADU regulations at 760 CMR 71.00 published January 31, 2025 and took effect February 2, 2025.

A "Protected Use ADU" is an attached or detached unit on a lot in a single-family zoning district where no other ADU exists. For those units, municipalities **may not**:

- require a special permit or public hearing
- require owner-occupancy of the ADU or the principal dwelling
- require more than one additional parking space, or any parking at all where the entire lot sits within a half mile of a transit station
- impose unreasonable restrictions on creation or on rental, except that short-term rental may be restricted or prohibited

Size is capped at 900 sq ft or 50% of the principal dwelling's floor area, whichever is smaller. Title 5 septic compliance is explicitly preserved as a reasonable regulation.

### 4.2 Uptake, first year

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| ADUs approved statewide, first year | 1,200+ |
| Applications reported by September 2025 | ~840 |
| State approvals by September 2025 | 550+ |

**Top ten communities by 2025 approvals:** Plymouth 34, Lawrence 32, Nantucket 27, Lowell 26, Milton 24, Somerville 24, Worcester 23, Methuen 21, Medford 19, Fairhaven 18.

That list is the whole strategy in one line. **Seven of the ten are sewered cities and inner suburbs. Three are coastal or seasonal.** Those are two different businesses.

### 4.3 Financing

MassHousing opened an ADU construction loan to consumers in spring 2026: a fixed-rate second mortgage financing **up to $250,000 for detached** and $150,000 for attached units, amortizing over 20 years, matched with an additional layer at zero percent interest with deferred repayment. Income limits run to 135% of AMI, reaching $205,335 in eastern Massachusetts. The property must be the borrower's primary residence and a single-family home.

**Caveat:** the pool is $5 million, which at $250,000 per loan funds roughly twenty units. As with the East End programs, do not build a pipeline assuming every prospect is funded.

### 4.4 Seasonal Communities

The Affordable Homes Act created a Seasonal Community designation. Forty-four municipalities now hold it, including nine Cape Cod towns (Brewster, Chatham, Dennis, Eastham, Harwich, Orleans, Provincetown, Truro, Wellfleet) and the six island towns. The first grant round awarded $1.9 million across 14 communities for FY2026, covering planning, zoning and permitting work plus **wastewater, utility and site improvements.** Eastham, Orleans, Chatham and Nantucket each received $175,000.

This is a municipal-capacity subsidy rather than a per-unit subsidy, but it directly funds the two things that gate ADU delivery in these towns: permitting throughput and wastewater infrastructure.

## 5. THE WASTEWATER CATCH

Do not assume leaving Suffolk County leaves Article 6 behind.

MassDEP amended 310 CMR 15.000 (Title 5) effective July 7, 2023 to cut nitrogen loading in Cape Cod coastal estuaries. **Thirty-one Cape watersheds are designated Natural Resource Nitrogen Sensitive Areas.** Within an NRNSA, systems must use Best Available Nitrogen Reducing Technology unless the municipality has filed for a watershed permit or qualifies for a de minimis exemption. Towns, not homeowners, apply for watershed permits, and those permits let a town substitute sewering, I/A systems, permeable reactive barriers, fertigation wells, wetland and cranberry bog restoration, or shellfish aquaculture.

This is structurally the same problem as Suffolk County, with one meaningful difference: **in Massachusetts the burden can shift to the municipality through a watershed permit, and Seasonal Communities grant money is explicitly available for wastewater work.** On the East End the burden sits entirely on the homeowner.

**The conclusion is the same as in the first memo: sewer availability is the single highest-value screening variable in this business, and it should be the first filter applied to any market or any individual lot.**

## 6. RANKED SHORTLIST

### Tier 1: execute here

**1. Cape Cod Seasonal Communities (nine towns)**
By-right 900 sq ft, no owner-occupancy mandate, no rent cap, Seasonal Community grant money flowing to permitting and wastewater, 36% of Cape housing stock seasonal, mainland logistics, and the identical seasonal-workforce narrative to the Hamptons. Constraint: NRNSA nitrogen in 31 watersheds. Screen lots for sewer and watershed permit status before quoting.

**2. Nantucket and Martha's Vineyard**
The closest analog to Sag Harbor anywhere. Nantucket median $3.7 million and Martha's Vineyard $1.4 million, both the highest in the state. 60% of island homes seasonal or short-term. Over half of island employers report losing workers or having offers declined. Nantucket delivered 27 ADU approvals in 2025, third in the Commonwealth. Historic district review is genuinely strict, which is a moat for a firm that can design to it rather than a barrier. Constraints: barge logistics, island labor premium, small absolute market.

**3. Sewered eastern Massachusetts (Lowell, Lawrence, Methuen, Worcester, Somerville, Medford, Milton)**
No septic at all, the highest permit volumes in the state, MassHousing financing, and the transit parking waiver inside a half mile. This is the volume play and the easiest possible execution. Constraint: it is a commodity market with real competition and no heritage story, so it rewards cost and cycle time rather than design.

### Tier 2: strong, with a specific reason

**4. Greater Portland, Maine**
LD 2003 by-right since July 2023, sewered urban core, strong rents, and critically **the Northeast modular supply chain is local**: KBS Builders in South Paris, Ecocor, BrightBuilt Home in Portland, GO Logic. Lowest manufacturing and freight cost in the region, which matters more than it sounds when modules are the product. Bar Harbor is a separate seasonal workforce market against Acadia.

**5. Newport, Rhode Island**
2024 state law, city proposing 900 sq ft for studios and one-bedrooms and 1,200 sq ft for two-bedrooms, sewered, acute seasonal workforce shortage, and a historic character story that rhymes with Sag Harbor. Constraint: Rhode Island's law is strong on paper but local implementation varies, and owner-occupancy is required.

**6. Vermont resort towns (Stowe, Woodstock, Killington)**
Act 47 by-right with a **30-day statutory approval clock**, which is the fastest entitlement path in the region, and **VHIP 2.0 pays $50,000 per ADU as an outright grant** against a 20% match. That is the best per-unit subsidy in the Northeast. Constraints: small market, lower rents, owner-occupancy tied to the lot.

**7. New Hampshire Seacoast, Lakes Region, North Conway**
HB 577 took effect July 1, 2025 and now mandates detached ADUs by right, one per lot, with any municipal size cap floored at 750 sq ft and generous conversion rights even where existing structures violate setbacks. Constraint: mostly septic outside the cities.

### Tier 3: watch, do not enter

**Connecticut.** Roughly 115 of 169 towns opted out of PA 21-29. HB 5507 in the 2026 session would repeal the opt-out and restore as-of-right ADUs statewide. If it passes, Fairfield County becomes immediately interesting given rent levels and proximity. Until then it is 115 separate negotiations.

**New Jersey.** No statewide preemption. S-2347 died in committee January 2026 and S1786 is pending. 564 municipalities each write their own rules.

**New York outside NYC.** No statewide preemption, which is precisely the East End's problem. NYC itself is a genuine opportunity on the strength of the March 18, 2026 Plus One reopening and the eleven pre-approved DOB-reviewed designs, but it is a dense-urban business, not a backyard-cottage business.

## 7. WHAT THE REGIME CHANGE DOES TO THE NUMBERS

### 7.1 Per-unit comparison, illustrative

| | East Hampton | Massachusetts seasonal | Massachusetts sewered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max detached size | 600 sq ft | 900 sq ft | 900 sq ft |
| Entitlement | Permit cap, annual renewal | By right, no hearing | By right, no hearing |
| Owner-occupancy | Required (Southampton) | Cannot be required | Cannot be required |
| Rent | Capped, $3,092 1BR | Market, uncapped | Market, uncapped |
| Wastewater | I/A OWTS, homeowner pays | NRNSA possible, town may hold watershed permit | **None** |
| All-in cost | ~$232,000 (360 sq ft base case) | ~$455,000 | ~$425,000 |
| Contractor gross margin at 20% | $46,000 | $91,000 | $85,000 |
| Plus coordination fee | $10,000 | $10,000 | $10,000 |
| **Contribution per unit** | **$56,000 to $66,000** | **~$101,000** | **~$95,000** |
| **Breakeven at $450k fixed overhead** | **~8 units/yr** | **~4.5 units/yr** | **~4.7 units/yr** |

*Illustrative. Massachusetts cost assumes 900 sq ft at approximately $400 per sq ft delivered plus site work and soft costs, with septic included in the seasonal column and excluded in the sewered column. Requires validation against factory quotes.*

**The larger permitted envelope is doing most of the work here.** A 900 sq ft unit costs roughly 80% more than a 360 sq ft unit and generates roughly 100% more contribution, because foundation, sanitary, utility connection, permit and mobilization are nearly identical at both sizes. Small units are the worst product in this business and the East End codes force you to build them.

### 7.2 The revenue ceiling difference

East Hampton caps a one-bedroom at $3,092 per month and a two-bedroom at $3,571, permanently, with no upside case. Massachusetts imposes no cap at all on a protected-use ADU that is not a short-term rental.

Nantucket rental data is noisy and seasonally distorted, so treat specific figures with caution. What is not in doubt is the structural point: **in Massachusetts the rent is whatever the market pays and it compounds with the market, and on the East End it is a number set by HUD.** Over a twenty-year hold that difference dominates every other variable in the homeowner's decision.

## 8. THE STRATEGIC READ

- **The East End is the hardest ADU market in the Northeast, and the reason anyone is trying to build a business there is that they are from there.** That is a real founder trap and also a real asset. Local credibility, a mayor who takes the meeting, and a design vocabulary earned by growing up in the place do not travel. The legal regime does.
- **The resolution is to prove in Sag Harbor and scale in Massachusetts.** Do the demonstration unit where the access is. But design the product to the Massachusetts envelope from day one, at 900 sq ft with a 600 sq ft variant for the East End codes, rather than designing a 360 sq ft unit to a New York constraint and then trying to stretch it.
- **Second market should be Cape Cod, not New York City.** The Cape keeps the entire narrative intact (seasonal community, essential workers, historic character, coastal environment), keeps the design language relevant, sits on the mainland, carries state grant money for exactly the constraints that bind, and operates under a by-right regime with no rent cap. New York City is a bigger market and a completely different business.
- **Nantucket and the Vineyard are the halo projects.** Small, expensive, logistically painful, design-reviewed to within an inch, and worth doing anyway for the portfolio and the press. Twenty-seven approvals on Nantucket in 2025 says the demand is not theoretical.
- **Screen for sewer first, always.** It is the single largest cost variance in the business and the fastest disqualifier. This holds in Barnstable County exactly as it holds in Suffolk County.
- **Expect competition wherever the law is good.** Massachusetts already has Any Season Design Build, Bensonwood in Walpole NH, Connor Homes in Middlebury VT, Acorn Deck House in Acton, Avalon Building Systems serving the Cape and islands, and DC Structures. The East End's emptiness is not a sign of opportunity, it is a sign of difficulty.

## 9. WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS VIEW

1. **Connecticut HB 5507.** If the PA 21-29 opt-out is repealed, Fairfield County becomes a top-tier market overnight on rent levels alone.
2. **A New York statewide ADU preemption.** None is pending, but it would transform the East End thesis from a boutique practice into a scalable business.
3. **Actual Massachusetts permit-to-completion data.** 1,200 approvals is an approval count, not a completion count. The conversion rate from approval to certificate of occupancy is the number that matters and it is not yet published.
4. **MassHousing pool expansion.** $5 million funds roughly twenty detached units. Whether the program is renewed and enlarged determines how financing-led the Massachusetts market becomes.
5. **Watershed permit status by Cape town.** A town holding a watershed permit is a materially cheaper place to build than one that does not. This is a town-by-town screen worth doing before any market entry.
