**Prepared:** September 23, 2026
**Subject:** Positioning, message, channel and offer design for the four ADU customer segments
**Companion to:** `market-research.md`, `plinth-labs-assessment.md`, `northeast-market-comparison.md`, `demand-and-use-cases.md`

---

## 1. WHAT REALLY MATTERS

- **This business can outspend every adjacent trade on customer acquisition, and that is the central strategic fact.** Residential solar carries a customer acquisition cost of $3,000 to $7,000 per sale, rising 40% to $0.84 per watt in 2026, against perhaps $8,000 to $12,000 of gross profit. An ADU contract carries roughly **$95,000 of contribution**. At the standard 3:1 LTV to CAC benchmark an ADU business could justify $31,000 per closed deal. **That means high-touch channels solar cannot afford (professional referral cultivation, seminar circuits, twelve-month nurture, in-home consultations) are comfortably economic here.** Do not compete on paid search against contractors.
- **Massachusetts has a second, unnoticed subsidy that is a perfect fit for the largest segment.** The Home Modification Loan Program lends **$1,000 to $50,000 at 0% interest with no monthly payments, deferred until sale or refinance**, under very broad income eligibility, and **accessory dwelling units are an explicitly eligible project.** Stacked with the MassHousing ADU loan of up to $250,000, an eldercare buyer can assemble $300,000 of favorable financing.
- **The distribution network for the eldercare segment already exists and is free.** Massachusetts has **350 municipal Councils on Aging** and 27 Aging Services Access Points. They are publicly funded, actively seeking programming for older residents, and constitutionally disposed to help. No competitor is using them.
- **In three of the four segments the buyer is not the occupant.** Eldercare is sold to the adult child, not the parent. Workforce is sold to the employer, not the tenant. Investor is sold to someone who will never set foot in the unit. Only downsize-in-place has buyer and occupant in the same person. **Marketing written to the occupant will miss the decision-maker in three cases out of four.**
- **Do not run four segments at once with a small team.** Pick eldercare, build the referral spine, add downsize-in-place second because the channels overlap almost completely.

## 2. THE ONE ASSET THAT SERVES ALL FOUR

Before any segment campaign, build a **feasibility screen**: address in, answer out.

- Is this lot eligible, and under what rule
- Is it sewered, or what is the wastewater path and cost
- What size unit can be built here
- What will it rent for, from local comparables
- What will it cost, as a real range
- Which programs stack, and what the net looks like

New York City proved the mechanism when it launched an eleven-design pre-approved plan library alongside a feasibility and cost tool in March 2026. A screen does four jobs at once: it qualifies leads at zero marginal cost, it disqualifies bad lots before they consume a site visit, it is the single best inbound content asset in the category, and it is the credibility demonstration in every professional referral conversation. **It is worth more than any advertising budget.**

## 3. SEGMENT 1 — ELDERCARE AND MULTIGENERATIONAL

**Roughly 61% of all ADUs built. Lead with this.**

### Who actually buys

Not the parent. **The adult child, typically 50 to 65, usually a daughter, often coordinating with siblings.** They hold the anxiety, they run the logistics, and in most cases they influence or control the money. The parent is the occupant and frequently the reluctant party.

### What they are actually buying

Not a building. They are buying a way out of the assisted living conversation, shorter caregiving logistics, their parent's dignity, and relief from a decision they feel guilty about either way.

### The message

> **Keep your mother close, not in a facility. For less than five years of what a facility costs, permanently.**

### The financial argument nobody else is making

Massachusetts assisted living runs a median of **$7,250 to $9,600 per month**, which is $87,000 to $115,200 a year. A $425,000 ADU breaks even in **3.7 to 4.9 years**.

But the sharper frame is this: **assisted living is a consumption expense that destroys the estate. An ADU is a capital improvement that preserves it.** A family staring at a potential Medicaid spend-down is not choosing between two housing options. They are choosing between burning $115,000 a year until the money is gone, and converting the same money into an asset that stays in the family and throws off $28,000 to $34,000 a year of rent afterward.

That is a wealth-preservation argument delivered to a wealth-preservation audience, and it is why elder law attorneys are the right channel rather than home-improvement advertising.

### The stack to present

| Source | Amount | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| Home Modification Loan Program (HMLP) | Up to $50,000 | **0%, no monthly payments, deferred to sale or refinance** |
| MassHousing ADU construction loan | Up to $250,000 detached | Fixed 2nd mortgage, 20-yr, plus a 0% deferred layer |
| **Combined** | **Up to $300,000** | Against a ~$425,000 project |

### Channels, ranked

1. **Elder law attorneys.** They own the estate and Medicaid planning conversation, they already maintain referral networks for senior housing, and they are the single highest-intent referral source in the category. Cultivate twenty of them.
2. **Geriatric care managers and Aging Life Care professionals.** They assess needs and coordinate placement. They are asked "what are our options" directly.
3. **The 350 Councils on Aging.** Run a free seminar: "Aging in Place: What the 2025 ADU Law Changed." These organizations need programming and will fill the room for you.
4. **HMLP regional administering agencies** (Way Finders, RCAP Solutions, Metro Housing Boston, SMOC, Berkshire Regional Planning). They process the loan that funds your product. Be the builder they know how to refer to.
5. **Hospital discharge planners and rehab social workers.** The trigger event is almost always acute.
6. **Financial advisors and CPAs** doing retirement and estate work.

### Trigger events to build the nurture around

A fall. A hospitalization. A dementia diagnosis. The death of a spouse. A parent's house going on the market. **The decision window after a trigger is short and the default is a facility.** The goal of all upstream channel work is simply to be the option that comes to mind in that two-week window.

### Objections and answers

- **"$425,000 is too much."** It is 44 months of assisted living at the median, and at the end of 44 months you still own the asset.
- **"Will this affect Medicaid eligibility or the spend-down?"** This is a real and complicated question. **Do not answer it yourself.** Have an elder law attorney on retainer to answer it, and make that referral part of the sales process. It is also precisely why attorneys are the right channel.
- **"What happens when my mother no longer needs it?"** It becomes a rental at roughly $28,000 to $34,000 of NOI, or you move into it yourself and rent the main house.

### Product implication

**Build accessibility in as standard, never as an upgrade.** Single level, zero-threshold entry, 36-inch doors, curbless shower, blocking in the bathroom walls for future grab bars, lever hardware, wider turning radius. It costs very little in the factory, it is required by 73% of owners who cite accessibility, and a unit that cannot take a wheelchair is unsellable to the largest segment in the market.

### What not to say

Nothing about the housing crisis. Nothing about sustainability or net zero. Nothing about solving anything for anybody else. This buyer has one problem and it is in their family.

## 4. SEGMENT 2 — DOWNSIZE IN PLACE

**Best pure economics in the category. Channels overlap heavily with Segment 1, so run it second and reuse the spine.**

### Who buys

Age 60 to 72, empty nester, owns a 3 or 4 bedroom house in a high-rent town, substantial equity, deeply attached to the neighborhood, doctors, church and friends.

### The insight to lead with

**In an expensive suburb, downsizing does not save money.** A two-bedroom condo in Newton costs roughly what their house is worth. Everyone in this position has run that math, discovered the plan is broken, and quietly given up on moving. They are stuck and they feel it.

### The message

> **Don't sell your house. Rent it. Move forty feet.**

And the legal hook, which almost nobody knows:

> **Since February 2025, Massachusetts forbids your town from requiring you to live in the main house.** You can build the new unit, move into it, and rent out the home you raised your family in.

### The numbers

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| 900 sq ft ADU, all-in, sewered | $425,000 |
| Rent on the vacated 3BR, Boston metro average | $4,007/mo, $48,084/yr |
| Less opex at 30% | ($14,425) |
| **Net operating income** | **$33,659** |
| **Unlevered yield on cost** | **7.9%** |
| MassHousing eligibility | **Yes.** It remains their primary residence |

They also avoid capital gains above the $500,000 exclusion, avoid brokerage and moving costs, and move into a new, efficient, zero-maintenance, fully accessible unit that will serve them for the rest of their lives.

### Channels, ranked

1. **Realtors, but counterintuitively.** A listing agent loses the commission if the client stays, so do not ask for live sellers. **Ask for their dead leads.** Every agent working the 60-plus market has a file of people who explored downsizing, discovered it did not pencil, and stopped returning calls. That file is the single best prospect list in this segment and it is currently worth nothing to the agent. Offer a referral fee on it.
2. **Financial advisors and wealth managers.** An asset throwing $33,659 a year is a bond substitute inside a retirement income plan, and advisors are actively looking for those.
3. **Councils on Aging.** Same seminar circuit as Segment 1, different back half of the talk.
4. **Direct mail and geo-targeted digital** to owners aged 60-plus, 20-plus years of tenure, three or more bedrooms, in towns where rents clear $3,300. This is unusually targetable from public assessor and deed data.
5. **Property managers,** who gain a unit to manage and lose nothing.

### Objections and answers

- **"I don't want to be a landlord."** Bundle property management into the offer and quote the net.
- **"I don't want strangers on my property."** You choose the tenant, you hold the lease, and they are in the building you left rather than the one you live in.
- **"The main house is too big to rent easily."** Family-sized rentals are the scarcest product in these towns and command the metro's highest rents.

## 5. SEGMENT 3 — INVESTOR

**Real, geographically narrow, and the least forgiving. Sell it with a spreadsheet, not a story.**

### Who buys

Small residential investors holding one to ten doors, plus homeowners crossing over into investing. They will get three bids, they will model it themselves, and they will not pay for design.

### The unlock to lead with

**Massachusetts cannot require owner-occupancy of either unit.** Most investors do not know this. It means an investor can buy a single-family, add a second income unit, and operate both without living there. It is the single most newsworthy fact you have for this audience and it should be the headline.

### The message

> **A second door on land you already own. No owner-occupancy requirement, by right, no public hearing.**

### Sell by disqualifying

Lead with the screen, publicly:

> **You need roughly $3,300 per month of achievable rent to clear a 6.5% unlevered yield at current build costs.**

| Market | 2BR rent | Yield at $425k | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newton | ~$4,400 | 7.2%+ | Works |
| Medford | $3,432 | 5.6% | Marginal |
| Somerville | $3,300 to $4,046 | 5.4% to 6.7% | Marginal to works |
| Lowell | $2,446 | 4.8% | **Fails** |
| Lawrence | $2,415 | 4.7% | **Fails** |
| Worcester | $2,156, **down 0.68%** | 4.3% | **Fails** |

Telling an investor their market does not work buys more credibility than any pitch, and it saves you the cost of chasing deals that die in underwriting.

### Solve the financing gap and you own the segment

**Every subsidized program requires primary residence.** MassHousing requires it. HMLP requires it. So the investor is on conventional, DSCR or construction-to-perm debt for the whole stack, which is exactly why a 4.3% Worcester yield is fatal rather than merely thin. **Whoever first lines up a lender doing construction-to-permanent or DSCR product specifically for Massachusetts ADUs will take this segment,** because nobody has productized it.

### Channels, ranked

1. **A public ADU yield map of Massachusetts**, town by town, showing achievable rent against build cost and resulting yield. Genuinely useful, highly linkable, and it generates qualified inbound while pre-disqualifying the rest. Best single marketing investment for this segment.
2. **Local real estate investor associations and meetups.** Speak, do not exhibit.
3. **DSCR and construction lenders,** co-marketing.
4. **Investment-focused realtors and small multifamily brokers.**
5. **BiggerPockets and the equivalent forums,** through genuinely useful analysis rather than promotion.

### Expect

Margin pressure, competitive bidding, no loyalty, and the fastest sales cycle of the four. Price accordingly and do not discount design work you are not being paid for.

## 6. SEGMENT 4 — EMPLOYER AND WORKFORCE

**Longest cycle, lowest volume, highest public relations value. This is enterprise business development, not marketing.**

### The structural problem

The employer wants the bed but does not own land. The homeowner owns land but does not want the risk. **You are standing up a three-sided market, and three-sided markets are hard.** Budget twelve to eighteen months per institutional deal.

### Change the ask

As established in the Plinth assessment, asking an employer to underwrite $425,000 of capex costs them roughly $29,500 a year amortized against a $12,000 housing stipend. They will not do it. **Ask for a five to ten year master lease instead.** No capital from the employer, operating expense rather than capital expense, and it solves their actual problem, which is a bed in July.

### Two messages, because there are two customers

**To the employer:**
> **You are not buying a building. You are guaranteeing a bed for five years, at a known annual cost, on someone else's balance sheet.**

**To the homeowner, and this is the part to exploit:**
> **A five-year corporate master lease. No tenant screening, no vacancy, no turnover, no collections, and a creditworthy counterparty.**

That is a genuinely **better product than a market rental** for a risk-averse homeowner, and it should be priced and sold as the premium option rather than the charitable one.

### Target the right employers

Restaurants have no balance sheet, no planning horizon and thin seasonal margins. They are the wrong first call despite being the loudest voice on the problem.

**Go to institutions instead:**

1. **Hospitals.** Nurse and technician retention, real budgets, multi-year planning, and an existing housing-benefit concept. A hospital master-leasing ten units is the largest single contract available in this business.
2. **School districts.** Teacher recruitment is a documented crisis in exactly these towns, and districts plan on multi-year cycles.
3. **Municipalities as employers.** Police, fire, DPW. Politically irresistible and they control the permitting.
4. **The fourteen designated Seasonal Communities,** which hold designation, grant money and dedicated staff. The FY26 round put $175,000 each into Eastham, Orleans, Chatham and Nantucket, explicitly usable for wastewater and permitting.
5. **Chambers of commerce.** The Cape Cod Chamber is already campaigning publicly on this and wants a solution to point at.
6. **Hotel and restaurant associations,** as a group rather than one by one.

### Why do it at all, given the effort

Three reasons, none of them volume. It generates the press and political goodwill that make permitting easier for every other segment. It is the story that wins Seasonal Communities grant support. And a single hospital or district contract is ten to twenty units at once, which no other segment delivers.

## 7. SUMMARY

| | Eldercare | Downsize | Investor | Employer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Share of market | ~61% | Not measured | Narrow | Smallest |
| Buyer | Adult child | The owner | Investor | Institution |
| Buying | Peace of mind | Income plus staying put | Yield | Staffing |
| Emotional or rational | Emotional | Both | Rational | Institutional |
| Price sensitivity | Lowest | Low | Highest | Low, but slow |
| Sales cycle | 2 to 6 months | 3 to 9 months | 1 to 3 months | 12 to 18 months |
| Primary channel | Elder law attorneys | Realtors' dead leads | Yield map and REIAs | Hospitals and districts |
| Financing | HMLP + MassHousing, to $300k | MassHousing to $250k | **None. Conventional only** | Employer master lease |
| Survives a soft rental market | **Yes** | Partly | **No** | Yes |
| Priority | **1** | 2 | 3 | 4 |

### Budget frame

At roughly $95,000 of contribution per contract, a 3:1 benchmark permits $31,000 of CAC. Do not spend it. Solar referral channels run $300 to $600 and partnership channels $500 to $1,000 per customer, and the same will hold here. **Target $3,000 to $6,000 per closed deal and put the money into professional relationships and the feasibility tool rather than into advertising.** The abnormally high contribution per unit is what makes seminars, long nurture and in-person referral cultivation economic, not what makes expensive clicks economic.

## 8. RISKS AND MITIGANTS

- **Risk: running all four segments at once fragments a small team across four channel strategies, four message architectures and four sales cycles.**
  **Mitigant:** eldercare only for the first year, then add downsize-in-place, which reuses roughly 70% of the same channels.
- **Risk: giving Medicaid, tax or eligibility advice in the eldercare sale creates real liability and will eventually be wrong.**
  **Mitigant:** retain an elder law attorney, refer every such question out, and make the referral a selling point rather than a deflection.
- **Risk: the investor segment collapses if Boston-area rents keep softening.** Vacancy has already moved from 3.40% to 4.90% and metro rent growth is +1.04%.
  **Mitigant:** never let investor deals exceed a minority of the pipeline, and hold the $3,300 screen even when the quarter is slow.
- **Risk: the employer segment consumes disproportionate founder time for negligible near-term revenue.**
  **Mitigant:** cap it at a fixed number of hours per month, pursue only institutions with real balance sheets, and treat the output as political capital rather than revenue.
- **Risk: realtors decline to refer clients who will not transact.**
  **Mitigant:** pay a real referral fee on closed contracts and target the dead-lead file specifically, which costs them nothing they currently value.
- **Risk: the feasibility tool gives a wrong answer and a customer builds toward it.**
  **Mitigant:** present it as an indicative screen with explicit disclaimers, require a paid site assessment before any contract, and never let the tool output a fixed price.
