# EAST END ADU AND INFILL DWELLINGS — MARKET RESEARCH (1 OF 1)

**Prepared:** September 18, 2026
**Subject:** Market, regulatory, and cost diligence for a lightweight, sustainable, architecturally driven dwelling company targeting ADUs and infill in Sag Harbor, the Hamptons, and beyond
**Status:** Research phase. No model or deck built yet.

---

## 1. WHAT REALLY MATTERS

- **The binding constraint is wastewater, not energy.** Suffolk County Sanitary Code Article 6 governs bedroom count, sanitary density, and whether a lot can carry a second dwelling at all. Energy independence, the explicit center of the stated thesis, does not relieve a single one of these constraints. The company's technical differentiation is pointed at the wrong bottleneck.
- **The subsidy stack is unusually deep and is the single best reason to enter now.** The East End Community Housing Fund crossed $100.74 million in collections through July 2026 (Southampton $58M, East Hampton $32.18M), funded by a voter-approved 0.5% real estate transfer tax layered on top of the existing 2% Community Preservation Fund tax. Southampton offers zero-interest ADU loans up to $125,000; East Hampton launched a $1 million pilot in 2026 offering ten no-interest, deferred-payment loans of up to $100,000. Suffolk County plus New York State septic grants reach a combined $45,000. That is $145,000 to $170,000 of non-dilutive capital per unit flowing to the customer, not the builder.
- **The local market is a hard ceiling, not a growth market.** East Hampton caps accessory dwelling permits at 40 per school district and 200 town-wide. Southampton requires 30,000 sq ft lots, relaxed to 20,000 sq ft only in census-designated places below 500 persons per square mile, which explicitly excludes Hampton Bays, East Quogue, Speonk, Quiogue, Riverside, and Shinnecock Hills. Sag Harbor Village currently requires 20,000 sq ft and 70 ft of lot width against a village stock containing lots as small as 5,000 sq ft.
- **Cost, not design, determines whether this business exists.** East Hampton caps detached units at 600 sq ft and caps rent at 130% of HUD Fair Market Rent (studio $2,589/mo, 1BR $3,092/mo for 2025 to 2026). That fixes the revenue side of the homeowner's decision. The product must land at or below roughly $325,000 all-in for 600 sq ft, or approximately $540/sq ft inclusive of septic, site work, and crane, for the homeowner to clear a 7% unlevered yield. California turnkey comps run $537/sq ft (Abodu, 500 sq ft) before a New York labor premium that pushes custom Hamptons work to $800 to $2,000/sq ft.
- **The prefab graveyard is the base rate.** Katerra ($2.4B raised, bankrupt 2021), Veev ($600M raised, unicorn valuation, liquidated 2023, assets bought by Lennar for single-digit millions), Entekra, Blu Homes, and Revolution Precrafted all failed on the same mechanism: fixed factory overhead against volatile, lumpy demand. Any plan that opens with owned manufacturing repeats the experiment.

The price-to-income ratio in Southampton Town exceeded 12x on 2018 data and has widened since. The demand is real, politically organized, and funded. The Long Island Association's East End advocacy agenda names housing cost and commute length as the primary constraint on staffing education, healthcare, tourism, and agriculture.

### 2.2 Demand is for tenancy, not ownership

Every East End ADU program restricts the unit to long-term rental occupancy. Southampton requires that both owner and tenant occupy year-round as principal residence and that at least one tenant work 30 hours per week at a business inside the town. East Hampton requires a one-year minimum lease and restricts tenancy to current residents, residents within the prior five years, or family members, with the unit serving as the tenant's sole legal residence. Short-term and summer rental is prohibited in both.

This matters commercially. The customer is a year-round homeowner making a 20-year landlord decision under a rent cap, not a second-home owner making an aesthetic purchase. The buying process is closer to a solar sale than a custom architecture sale: financing-led, payback-driven, and subsidy-dependent.

### 3.2 The Southampton density paradox

Southampton's 20,000 sq ft relief applies only where census density falls below 500 persons per square mile. Eligible: Bridgehampton, Eastport, Flanders, North Sea, Noyac, Water Mill, Tuckahoe. Excluded: Hampton Bays, East Quogue, Speonk, Quiogue, Riverside, Shinnecock Hills.

The relief is inversely correlated with where workforce tenants need to live. The hamlets with the most walkable service employment, the shortest commutes, and the most renters are precisely the ones zoned out of the small-lot exception. Any go-to-market plan that assumes the relief expands the addressable base in the dense hamlets is reading the law backwards.

### 3.3 Sag Harbor Village is the hardest jurisdiction and the best brand asset

The village is running a live workshop process on its ADU law, driven by the recognition that its 20,000 sq ft and 70 ft width thresholds block most village lots, some as small as 5,000 sq ft. Separately, most village work routes through the Board of Historic Preservation and Architectural Review, a five-member board that reviews aesthetic and safety changes including new points of egress. The village has also enacted the Historically Black Beachfront Communities Overlay District covering the Sag Harbor Hills, Azurest, and Ninevah subdivisions (SANS), listed on the National Register in July 2019, explicitly to preserve community character and scale while protecting long-term owner equity.

Reading this against the stated thesis of "respect for local character and cultural heritage": that language is not decoration in Sag Harbor, it is the approvals path. A design vocabulary that reads as contextual to the BHPAR is worth more in this market than any energy specification. The SANS overlay in particular is a place where a small, scale-appropriate, owner-equity-preserving dwelling product has a genuine constituency, and where a tone-deaf modernist box would be dead on arrival.

### 3.4 Suffolk County Article 6 is the real gate

Article 6 of the Suffolk County Sanitary Code governs groundwater protection through minimum lot size and Population Density Equivalent. Density is calculated at 300 gallons per day per acre in Groundwater Management Zone III and 600 gpd per acre in Zone IV, applied to adjusted gross lot area. The East End sits largely in the more restrictive zones.

An I/A OWTS nitrogen-reducing system is triggered when any of the following apply:
- the existing system lacks prior SCDHS approval, is a cesspool, is block rather than precast concrete, or is in poor condition
- bedroom count exceeds the prior approval
- the project is "major reconstruction," defined as cost exceeding 50% of appraised structure value
- an attached ADU increases gross floor area
- a detached ADU cannot connect to the existing system while holding required setbacks

In practice nearly every detached ADU on an older East End lot trips at least one of these. The relevant filing is SCDHS Form WWM-105, and bedroom counting follows SCDHS Memorandum #19. I/A OWTS units treat to 19 mg/L nitrogen, roughly 70% removal.

**Cost:** conventional system approximately $5,000; I/A OWTS $25,000 or more installed. Combined State and County grants reach up to $45,000 as of July 2025, composed of a base grant plus up to $10,000 additional for a pressurized shallow drain field ($5,000) and low-to-moderate income eligibility ($5,000). East Hampton separately routes homeowners to Water Quality Improvement Grants.

**Strategic read:** wastewater is where the product should be innovating. A dwelling delivered with a pre-engineered, SCDHS-pre-reviewed sanitary package, sized and permitted as part of the module rather than as a separate sitework scope, removes the single largest source of schedule risk, cost variance, and customer abandonment on the East End. That is a defensible moat. Solar and batteries are not.

### 3.5 New York State code

- **All-Electric Buildings Act.** For permits filed on or after December 31, 2025 (effectively January 1, 2026), new buildings of seven stories or fewer must be all-electric, with narrow exemptions where grid service would take 18 months or more longer than a mixed-fuel path. Emergency backup generators are not restricted. This is tailwind: it mandates the heat-pump, all-electric envelope the product would build anyway, and it removes gas-fired competitors from the comparison set.
- **Factory Manufactured Buildings (modular).** Governed by 19 NYCRR Part 1209. Each module carries a Department of State Insignia of Approval affixed at the factory, with plans approved via DOS Form 1280 and inspection by a DOS-approved third-party Quality Assurance Agency. Critically, the insignia travels with the unit, so a module built in Maine or Pennsylvania ships into Suffolk County pre-approved for structural, mechanical, electrical, and plumbing scope. Local jurisdiction retains zoning, siting, foundation, and utility connection. **This is the single most important fact for the operating model: New York's modular program makes an asset-light, contract-manufactured entry fully legal and fully competitive with owned manufacturing.**
- **Energy incentives.** NYSERDA residential storage rebate at $200/kWh upstate and $250/kWh in Con Edison territory. New York State solar tax credit at 25% of system cost capped at $5,000, Form IT-255, five-year carryforward. Federal ITC at 30%, now available to standalone storage. New York is transitioning net metering to the Value of Distributed Energy Resource (VDER) Value Stack, currently phase two, with net metering still electable. The 2025 NYS Fire Code requires peer review of battery energy storage systems above capacity thresholds, and local AHJs frequently lack in-house capacity to review BESS submittals, which adds permitting time.

**Stacked, a qualifying Southampton homeowner can access $170,000 or more of zero-interest or non-repayable capital against a project the company prices at roughly $325,000.** The customer's out-of-pocket falls toward $155,000 against $25,000 to $31,000 of annual gross rent. That is the sale. The company is not selling architecture, it is selling a financed, subsidy-assembled income stream, and the sales function must be able to underwrite and paper the stack.

**Caution:** program funding is annual, competitive, and small. East Hampton's 2026 pilot is ten loans. Southampton allotted $1.25 million to its program, implying roughly ten to twelve loans at the $125,000 cap. Plus One has been closed to new applicants since February 2024 and reopened on a limited basis in 2026. Pipeline must not be built on the assumption that every prospect is funded.

At an estimated 30 to 80 detached units per year across the whole East End and a $300,000 average contract, the **entire South Fork ADU market is $9M to $24M of annual revenue across all competitors.** A new entrant capturing 20% to 30% in years three to five is looking at $2M to $7M of revenue. That is a viable design-build practice. It is not, on its own, a venture-scale manufacturing business, and it cannot amortize an owned factory.

The base case sells itself. The downside case does not sell at all. ### 7.2 Why the arbitrage is real here specifically

The Hamptons labor premium is the largest in the country and the factory is not subject to it. A module built in South Paris, Maine or Scranton, Pennsylvania carries Maine or Pennsylvania labor cost, ships with a New York DOS insignia, and lands on a Suffolk County foundation having consumed perhaps 15% of the site labor hours a stick-built unit would. **The wider the local labor premium, the larger the modular arbitrage. The East End is therefore one of the best modular arbitrage markets in the United States, notwithstanding its small volume.** This is the most compelling single fact in the research.

## 8. TESTING THE "TECHNOLOGICALLY INDEPENDENT" THESIS

The stated vision is decentralized housing with self-sufficient energy systems operating "without reliance on large-scale infrastructure." Three findings bear on this.

- **Energy independence is technically achievable and commercially expensive.** A 600 sq ft all-electric unit under the All-Electric Buildings Act carries a heat pump, heat pump water heater, and induction cooking. Islanding that load through a Long Island winter requires roughly 6 to 9 kW of PV and 20 to 40 kWh of storage, an estimated $40,000 to $70,000 of equipment before incentives. Against a $325,000 cost target, full off-grid capability consumes 12% to 21% of the budget and breaks the homeowner's yield math.
- **Grid-tied with storage captures most of the benefit at a fraction of the cost.** NYSERDA storage rebates, the 30% federal ITC on standalone storage, the 25% NYS solar credit, and VDER or net metering export value all require grid interconnection. Going off-grid forfeits every one of them. Resilience and near-zero operating cost, delivered grid-tied, is the defensible claim. Full autonomy should be a priced option tier for remote or interconnection-constrained sites, not the base product.
- **Energy independence does not relieve the actual constraint.** Nothing in Article 6, the town permit caps, the lot size minimums, the rent caps, or the BHPAR review is affected by how the dwelling makes electricity. **If the company wants a technology thesis that unlocks supply rather than decorating it, the thesis is decentralized wastewater, not decentralized energy.** An integrated, pre-engineered, SCDHS-pre-reviewed I/A OWTS package delivered with the module attacks the binding constraint, the largest cost variance, and the longest schedule item simultaneously.

## 9. STRATEGIC READ

- **Entry model:** asset-light. Design and sell in-house, contract-manufacture with an existing DOS-insignia'd Northeast factory (KBS Builders in South Paris ME at 150,000+ sq ft capacity, Westchester Modular in Wingdale NY, Simplex Homes in Scranton PA), self-perform or subcontract site work and sanitary. Zero factory capex, and the failure base rate in Section 6.3 is almost entirely a factory-overhead failure.
- **Wedge product:** a single 600 sq ft detached one-bedroom, designed to the intersection of the binding caps (East Hampton's 600 sq ft detached maximum, Southampton's 400 sq ft minimum and 1,000 sq ft maximum, two-bedroom sanitary limits), all-electric by default, in two or three exterior vocabularies tuned to survive BHPAR and SANS overlay review. One SKU, three skins. Not a catalogue.
- **The real product is the permit.** Pre-approved plans are the proven accelerant: New York City's March 2026 launch of an 11-design DOB-reviewed library with simplified permitting is the template. Getting a standard design pre-reviewed at SCDHS and pre-conferenced with Southampton, East Hampton, and Sag Harbor building departments converts a 12-to-18-month owner odyssey into a defined product with a delivery date. That, not the architecture, is what the customer is buying.
- **Sequence the geography.** The East End is the brand beachhead and the reference portfolio, not the business. Sag Harbor's constraints make it the hardest possible proving ground, which is exactly why clearing it is credible everywhere else. Second market is New York City outer boroughs on the same state code and the same Plus One architecture. Third is the balance of Long Island and the Hudson Valley.
- **Own the capital stack as a service.** The buying decision is financing-led. A team that can assemble CHF loans, Plus One, septic grants, NYSERDA rebates, and the ITC into a single underwritten package will out-convert better architects. This is the commercial moat in a market this small.

## 10. RISKS AND MITIGANTS

- **Risk: statutory permit caps hard-limit local volume.** East Hampton is capped at 200 town-wide and 40 per school district.
  **Mitigant:** treat the East End as a reference market with a defined ceiling, underwrite the business plan on out-of-market expansion from year two, and engage early in Sag Harbor's live ADU code review where the thresholds are still being written.
- **Risk: subsidy programs are annual, competitive, and thinly funded.** East Hampton's 2026 pilot is ten loans; Southampton's allocation is $1.25 million; Plus One was closed to new applicants for two years.
  **Mitigant:** design the base product to pencil at 5% unlevered yield without any subsidy, and treat the stack as upside that accelerates close rates rather than as a requirement.
- **Risk: Article 6 sanitary triggers blow the cost budget and the schedule.** I/A OWTS at $25,000+, plus setback-driven redesign, is the largest single source of variance.
  **Mitigant:** pre-qualify every lead with a parcel-level sanitary screen before quoting, price the sanitary package inside the contract rather than as an allowance, and pursue a pre-reviewed standard sanitary detail with SCDHS.
- **Risk: historic and architectural review delays or kills designs.** BHPAR review plus the SANS overlay adds discretionary approval risk in the exact market the brand is built on.
  **Mitigant:** develop exterior vocabularies with a preservation consultant before the first sale, seek informal BHPAR pre-review of the standard designs, and publish the contextual design rationale as a marketing asset rather than fighting the constraint.
- **Risk: factory partner concentration.** Contract manufacturing means a single point of failure in schedule, quality, and price.
  **Mitigant:** qualify two DOS-insignia'd manufacturers before the first order, hold the design in a manufacturer-agnostic specification, and retain the DOS Form 1280 plan approval in the company's own name where permissible.
- **Risk: the market is too small to support a design-led cost structure.** A $2M to $7M revenue business cannot carry a large studio.
  **Mitigant:** one SKU with limited variants, no bespoke design work in the first 20 units, and revenue from permit and financing coordination services alongside the unit sale.
- **Risk: full off-grid specification destroys the yield math and forfeits incentives.**
  **Mitigant:** ship grid-tied with solar and storage as standard, price full autonomy as an option, and market resilience and operating cost rather than independence.
- **Risk: rent caps limit homeowner revenue permanently.** 130% of HUD FMR is the ceiling regardless of market rent.
  **Mitigant:** underwrite to the cap, never to market rent, and lead the sale with the yield-on-cash figure rather than a market-rent comparison the homeowner cannot legally capture.

## 11. WHAT IS STILL UNKNOWN

These are the gaps that primary-source diligence needs to close before any model or capital raise.

1. **Parcel-level addressable lot count.** GIS analysis of Southampton, East Hampton, and Sag Harbor Village tax parcels against lot size, existing unit count, CDP density eligibility, and setback capacity. This is the only way to size the market honestly and it is a two-week job with the Suffolk County parcel layer.
2. **Actual permit issuance history.** Southampton and East Hampton Building Departments hold the count of accessory apartment permits issued per year and how far East Hampton has consumed its 200-unit cap. Not published; requires a records request or a call to Kara Bak, Southampton Director of Housing and Community Development, at 631-702-1745.
3. **Sag Harbor Village ADU code outcome.** The village is actively revising lot size and width thresholds. The outcome materially changes the village-level opportunity.
4. **Southampton Town code as currently adopted.** The 30,000 / 20,000 sq ft thresholds and the 35%-of-principal size formula come from a secondary guide (Haven ADUs) and a 27East report; ecode360 blocked direct retrieval. Verify Article IIA text directly before relying on any of it.
5. **Real delivered cost from a Northeast factory.** Request budget pricing for a 600 sq ft, all-electric, DOS-insignia'd module from KBS Builders, Westchester Modular, and Simplex Homes. Everything in Section 7 depends on this number.
6. **SCDHS pre-review feasibility.** Whether the department will pre-review a standard sanitary detail for a repeat ADU design, and what that process costs and takes.
7. **PSEG Long Island storage incentive rate.** NYSERDA publishes $200/kWh upstate and $250/kWh Con Edison; the Long Island rate and interconnection terms need confirmation.
8. **Property tax treatment of the improvement.** Assessment uplift on the ADU directly reduces homeowner NOI and is not modeled here.
